# Best Sea to Sky — Brand Book

*The Local's Guide to the Sea to Sky Corridor*

**Version 1.0 · August 2026**

---

## Introduction

This is the reference for how Best Sea to Sky looks and sounds — the logo, the colours, the type, and the voice. It exists so that a Facebook post, an email footer, a printed rack card, and a listing page all read as the same thing: a guide written by people who actually live on this corridor.

Everything in here is drawn from what already ships on bestseatosky.com. Where the live site currently contradicts a rule, that's recorded honestly in **Known Deviations** at the end rather than papered over — those are the fix list, not the standard. When you build something new, this document is what you check against.

Two rules govern everything that follows:

1. **We sound like a neighbour, not a platform.** If a sentence could have come from a tourism board or a review aggregator, rewrite it.
2. **We don't fake anything.** No paid placements dressed as recommendations, no stock imagery standing in for a place it isn't, no claims we can't stand behind.

## Voice & Tone

### The core instruction

Write like a local telling a friend where to go. Confident, warm, specific, and slightly impatient with corporate travel-speak.

The site's own lines are the best calibration:

> "Every business vetted by people who live and eat and hike here"

> "You're here because you're good, not because you paid"

> "Built by your neighbours, not a tech company in California"

> "Stop scrolling through thousands of bot-written reviews and outdated tourist traps"

> "Insider knowledge you won't find in a brochure"

> "See the waterfalls, viewpoints, and parks that most tourists drive right past"

Notice what these have in common: short, plain words. A clear us-and-them. Concrete nouns — patios, trailheads, farm stands — never "experiences" or "offerings."

### Voice attributes

**Local.** We use first person plural and we mean it. "We live here." Reference real places by name. Specificity is the whole product.

**Direct.** Short sentences. Active voice. Say the thing. "Their loss. Your gain." is on-brand; "we strive to deliver curated excellence" is not.

**Warm, not cute.** Friendly and generous, but never twee. No puns on "sky-high." No exclamation marks stacked up.

**Honest about the competition.** We name Google Maps, TripAdvisor, and the tourism boards and explain the tradeoff fairly. We criticise the model — algorithms, pay-to-play, fake reviews — not individual businesses.

**Canadian.** Always Canadian spelling: **neighbour**, **favourite**, **colour**, **centre**, **kilometre**. This is not a detail. It signals immediately that we're from here.

### Say this, not that

| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| Places to stay | Accommodation options |
| The best post-trail patios | Premier dining experiences |
| Locals actually recommend | Highly rated by our community |
| Hand-picked | Curated selection of premium partners |
| We live here | Our team is passionate about the region |
| Hidden waterfalls | Must-see natural attractions |
| Neighbours, favourite, colour | Neighbors, favorite, color |

### Tone by context

- **Category and listing pages** — practical and useful. Lead with what it is and why it's worth your time.
- **Guides and itineraries** — the insider voice at full strength. This is where personality lives.
- **The "Why" and "Get Listed" pages** — the argument. Direct, comparative, unapologetic.
- **Email and newsletter** — one friend to another. Short. One clear thing to do.
- **Social** — the most casual register. Still never sloppy, still Canadian spelling.

### Things we never do

- Never claim a business is "the best" without the review data to back it
- Never run a paid placement without labelling it — affiliate links carry `rel="nofollow sponsored"`
- Never use stock photography of a place that isn't actually the one being written about
- Never write in the promotional register of a tourism board
- Never use American spelling

## Logo

### The mark

A circle containing three green mountains with white snow caps above layered blue water — the corridor itself, sea to sky, in one image. A soft grey ring encloses it.

**Colours within the mark:**

| Element | Hex |
|---|---|
| Outer ring | `#C8CDD0` |
| Sky field | `#F0F4F3` |
| Left mountain | `#5A8A3C` |
| Centre mountain | `#6A9E42` |
| Right mountain | `#7FB44E` |
| Snow caps | `#FFFFFF` |
| Deep water | `#1E5A8A` |
| Mid wave | `#2878B5` |
| Light wave | `#3A95CC` (70% opacity) |
| Wave accent line | `#5AB0DC` (60% opacity) |

These greens and blues belong to the mark only. They are **not** the interface palette — don't pull `#7FB44E` into a button.

### The lockup

The horizontal lockup is the icon followed by the wordmark:

- Icon at 36 × 36 px, gap of 10 px, wordmark at 20 px
- **Best** in Slate 900 `#0F172B`, **SeaToSky** in Emerald 700 `#007A55`
- Set in DM Serif Display. Note that only weight 400 is loaded — DM Serif Display ships no true bold, so `font-bold` renders as synthetic faux-bold. It's acceptable on screen at this size; for print or large-format, outline the wordmark from a proper vector file rather than relying on the browser.
- The wordmark is one closed string — `BestSeaToSky` — with camel-case capitals. In running text, always write it as three words: *Best Sea to Sky*.

### Usage rules

- **Clear space.** Keep at least the width of the icon's ring free on all sides. Nothing crowds the mark.
- **Minimum size.** 24 px for the full icon. Below that the snow caps and wave lines collapse — switch to `logo-small.svg`, the simplified single-mountain mark built for exactly this (see the file inventory below).
- **Never** stretch, skew, rotate, or recolour the mark.
- **Never** place the mark on a busy photo without a solid or heavily dimmed backing.
- **On dark backgrounds** — including the deep-green hero — use the mark on its own light circle. Do not knock it out to a single flat colour.
- **Favicon** is the icon alone, never the lockup.
- **Alt text** is always `Best Sea to Sky`.

### The logo package

The master asset set is the **best-sea-to-sky-logo-package** — eight vector variants, each in SVG, PDF, and EPS, plus pre-rendered PNGs. The wordmark in every lockup is true outlined DM Serif Display (with kerning), so nothing depends on fonts being installed, and the faux-bold problem doesn't apply to these files.

| Variant | Light | Dark | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full mark | `logo-icon.svg` | (self-contained) | Screen use ≥ 24 px, favicon, avatars |
| Simplified mark | `logo-small.svg` | (self-contained) | Below 24 px: 16 px favicons, watermarks, embroidery |
| Horizontal lockup | `logo-horizontal.svg` | `logo-horizontal-dark.svg` | Header, documents, email |
| Stacked lockup | `logo-stacked.svg` | `logo-stacked-dark.svg` | Square formats: social posts, stickers |
| Wordmark only | `wordmark.svg` | `wordmark-dark.svg` | Tight spaces, footers |

The dark variants set **Best** in white and **SeaToSky** in Emerald 300 `#5EE9B5` — the on-dark standard defined below. Print from the PDF or EPS files, never from a PNG. There is no `.ai` master yet; Illustrator opens SVG, PDF, and EPS directly, so a designer can save one from these.

Note on the Dropbox logo folder: the connected `473 pipe up` folder contains **Pipe-Up's** logo files, not Best Sea to Sky's. This package is the Best Sea to Sky master — there is no older vendor package to reconcile with.

### On light and dark

The mark carries its own white background inside the ring, so it sits cleanly on both. The wordmark does not. The footer currently sets the whole wordmark flat in Slate 200 `#E2E8F0`, dropping the two-tone split entirely.

**Recommended standard going forward:** on dark backgrounds set **Best** in white and **SeaToSky** in Emerald 300 `#5EE9B5`, preserving the split that makes the lockup recognisable. This is a change from what ships today — see Known Deviations.

- Emerald is the only accent that appears site-wide. Category colours stay inside their category.
- Never set body copy in a category colour.

The hex values in this book are accurate sRGB conversions, but on wide-gamut displays the rendered colour will be slightly more saturated than the hex suggests. Match to a printed proof, never to your screen — especially for the greens.

## Typography

### The pairing

| Role | Typeface | Weight | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Headings & wordmark** | DM Serif Display | 400 (and bold in the wordmark) | Google Fonts |
| **Body & UI** | Source Sans 3 | Regular, semibold, bold | Google Fonts |

Both are free from Google Fonts, both are already loaded on the site with `display: swap`. The fallback stacks are `Georgia, serif` and `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif`.

Two families is the limit. Do not introduce a third for a campaign, a slide deck, or a printed piece.

### How to use them

- **DM Serif Display** does the emotional work — page titles, section headers, subheads, the wordmark, pull quotes, and card titles. It has warmth and a bit of editorial weight, which is exactly the "written by a person" signal we want. Never set running body copy in it.
- **Source Sans 3** does everything else — body, navigation, buttons, captions, forms, tables. It's quiet and highly legible at small sizes, which matters because most of the site is people scanning listings on a phone.

The site uses DM Serif Display for headings all the way down to card titles at 14–16 px. That works at those sizes on screen, but it's the floor — don't go smaller, and don't use it for anything longer than a line or two.

### Hierarchy

| Level | Face | Size (desktop) | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero H1 | DM Serif Display | 48 px (36 px mobile) | White on dark, with emerald gradient emphasis |
| Page H1 | DM Serif Display | 36–40 px | Slate 900 |
| Section H2 | DM Serif Display | 28–32 px | Slate 900 |
| Subsection H3 | DM Serif Display | 20–22 px | Slate 900 |
| Card / inline title | DM Serif Display | 14–16 px | Slate 900 |
| Body | Source Sans 3 | 16–18 px | Slate 600 |
| Supporting / meta | Source Sans 3 | 14 px | Slate 500 |

Long-form pages use a `.prose` wrapper that sets 1.25em paragraph spacing and generous heading margins — apply it to guides, blog posts, and any page that's mostly reading. Listing and category pages space paragraphs with utility margins instead, which is fine; just don't mix both on one page or the spacing compounds.

### Type rules

- Headings are sentence case or title case — never all caps, never letter-spaced small caps.
- Keep line length to roughly 65–75 characters. Long measures kill listing pages.
- One emphasis technique per block: bold *or* colour, not both.
- Legibility beats styling everywhere, print included.

## Imagery

### What we show

Real places on this corridor, shown as they actually are. The trailhead, the patio at 4pm, the actual dining room. Our credibility rests on the place in the photo being the place in the text — generic mountain stock pretending to be somewhere it isn't quietly undermines that.

### Rules

- **Stock imagery is fine when it's genuinely the place being written about** — e.g. a licensed Dreamstime photo of the actual Shannon Falls or the actual Stawamus Chief. Verify the location before using it; a caption should never claim a photo shows a spot it doesn't. Original photography is still preferred where you have it — it's more specific and it's free — but stock is an acceptable fallback, not a compromise, as long as it's the real place.
- **No stock of a lookalike or generic mountain standing in for a specific Sea-to-Sky spot.** If you can't find or license a real photo of the actual location, use stock for texture and abstraction instead (a generic forest-path detail shot, a coffee cup, a highway line) rather than implying it's "here."
- Prefer daylight and natural weather over heavy grading. Grey days are honest here.
- Show people at human scale — small in the landscape, or genuinely mid-activity. Not posed.
- Every image needs descriptive alt text. It's an accessibility requirement and it earns SEO.
- Compress before upload. Page speed is a ranking factor and most visitors are on mobile, often on patchy corridor signal.

### Open Graph images

Each category has its own OG image (`og-eat.jpg`, `og-stay.jpg`, `og-play.jpg`, `og-visit.jpg`, `og-shop.jpg`, `og-services.jpg`), plus `og-default.jpg` for everything else.

**The spec is 1200 × 630.** The current JPGs are actually 889 × 470 while the code declares 1200 × 630 — they should be re-exported at full size (`og-default.svg` is the 1200 × 630 source template). Undersized OG images get upscaled by Facebook and LinkedIn and look soft in the feed.

When you add a category or a major landing page, add a matching OG image — a shared link with a wrong or missing preview looks careless and costs clicks.

### Shot lists by category

What "real places, photographed as they are" means in practice. Each list is the minimum set for a category page or a social series about one listing. Golden hour is nice; 4pm on a grey Tuesday is honest — both are on-brand.

**Eat 🍽️**

- The dish that people actually order, shot at the table, natural light, no styling
- The patio or dining room with people in it, mid-service — not empty at 10am
- The exterior with enough context to recognise it from the street
- The human detail: the chalkboard menu, the dog bowl by the door, the stack of firewood by the pizza oven
- For breweries: the flight on the table with the tanks or the mountain out the window behind it

**Stay 🏔️**

- The room as a guest first sees it from the doorway — not the wide-angle real-estate distortion
- The view from the window or deck, shot from inside so the frame shows you're *in* the room
- The detail that sells it: the wood stove, the hot tub steaming in the cold, the coffee setup
- The approach — driveway, trailhead access, or walk from the village
- One seasonal counterpart: the same exterior in snow and in summer

**Play ⛷️**

- Mid-activity at human scale — a climber small on the Chief, a rider halfway down the line, not a posed summit shot
- The trailhead or put-in, signage legible, so the photo is *useful*
- Gear-level detail: skis on the rack, chalk bags, the bike leaned at the viewpoint
- The reward: the patio beer, the view at the top, the après scene
- Weather honesty — at least one shot in cloud, rain, or flat light per set

**Visit 🌲**

- The classic view, but composed with a person in it for scale
- The approach path — what the walk in actually looks like, mud included
- The off-angle nobody shoots: the waterfall from downstream, the viewpoint looking back at the parking
- Season and time variants of the signature shot, because visitors arrive year-round
- The "drive right past" frame: what the highway pullout looks like so people don't miss it

**Shop 🛍️**

- Storefront with signage, shot from across the street
- Interior aisle or display at eye level, staff or customers present
- Product in hand — one item, held, natural light
- The maker where relevant: the potter at the wheel, the print rack at the market stall

**Services 🧭**

- The person doing the work: the guide mid-briefing, the outfitter fitting a harness
- The equipment that signals competence — tidy rope room, the drift boat, the van fleet
- Client-eye view: what you see when you show up for the tour
- The credential moment: certifications on the wall, the safety talk — trust is the product here

### Mockups

Mockups show the brand in a real setting before you commit to printing — the logo on a rack card, a sticker on a laptop, the site on a phone in someone's hand. Use them to check two things: does the mark hold up at that size, and does it still read as us in that context. Always mock up before a print run.

## File Types & Asset Management

### The formats and what each is for

| Format | What it is | Use for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SVG** | Web-friendly vector | Website, favicon, apps — the primary logo format | Not accepted by all printers |
| **PNG** | Raster with transparency | Presentations, overlays, digital placements on colour | Doesn't scale up; not for large print |
| **JPG** | Compressed raster | Photography, OG images, email | No transparency; degrades on re-save |
| **AI** | Editable Illustrator source | Handing to a designer for new assets | Requires Illustrator |
| **EPS** | Universal print vector | Large-format print, signage, banners | Legacy format; confirm your printer wants it |
| **PDF** | Portable, print-ready | Sending finished pieces to print | Fix fonts by embedding or outlining |

**Quick rule:** anything on a screen, use SVG or PNG. Anything printed, use a vector — AI, EPS, or PDF. Never scale up a JPG or PNG and hope.

### Storage

Keep one master folder as the single source of truth, with the logo package in all formats, the current colour and type reference, OG image templates, and approved photography. Everyone who makes anything for Best Sea to Sky pulls from there — not from a Slack message, not from a website screenshot.

Version by date, keep superseded files in an `_archive` subfolder rather than deleting them, and make sure at least one other person has access.

## Applying the Brand

### Website

- Logo top-left in the header, linked home, sticky on scroll.
- Favicon is `icon.svg`.
- Emerald 700 for all links and primary buttons; category colour only within its own section.
- Every page needs a title, a meta description, and a canonical URL.
- Affiliate and sponsored links always carry `rel="nofollow sponsored"` and a visible label. This is the pay-to-play promise — breaking it quietly costs more than the commission is worth.

### Social media

Currently active on Facebook (Best Sea to Sky) and Instagram (@bestseatosky).

- Profile picture is the icon alone — the lockup is illegible at avatar size.
- Cover images use the hero gradient with a single DM Serif Display line.
- Lead with the specific place, not the category: "The best post-trail patio in Squamish" beats "Check out our Eat section."
- Use the category emoji as a consistent signal.
- Keep the same handle, avatar, and bio across every platform. If you add LinkedIn, TikTok, or Pinterest later, update all of them the same week.
- Watermark original photography with the icon, bottom-right, at low opacity.

### Email

- Emerald 700 `#007A55` buttons, white text, 12 px radius.
- One clear action per email.
- Logo in the header, plain-text-feeling body. It should read like a note, not a campaign.
- Email clients don't load webfonts reliably, so a system sans stack in email is acceptable — it's the one place the two-family rule bends.

Note: the current subscribe-confirmation template is still on the old Tailwind v3 palette (`#047857` buttons, `#0f172a` / `#475569` / `#94a3b8` text). Those are visibly different colours from the site's v4 values and should be updated to the hexes in this book.

### Print & merchandise

- Convert to CMYK; expect the greens to shift and ask for a proof.
- Vector only — the PDF or EPS files from the logo package — for cards, stickers, banners, and apparel.
- On dark garments, use the mark on its light circle rather than knocking it out.
- Test contrast on the actual material. What reads on coated stock can disappear on kraft or fabric.
- Rack cards and stickers are the highest-value formats for a corridor audience — visitors pick them up in person, at the exact moment they're deciding where to go.

#### Garments

| | Approved | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Colours** | Forest (match to `#1A3A2A`), black, white, natural/cream, heather grey | The mark's greens and blues read on all five |
| **Avoid** | Emerald, green, or blue garments | They fight the mark's own colours; the logo disappears into them |
| **Dark garments** | Stacked lockup, dark variant — or the mark on its printed light circle | Never knock the mark out to a single flat colour |
| **Light garments** | Stacked or horizontal lockup, light variant | |

Left-chest hits are the stacked lockup at 70–80 mm tall or the icon alone at 60 mm. Full-front prints use the stacked lockup at 200–250 mm.

#### Print methods

| Method | Use for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Screen print** | T-shirts, totes, larger runs | The mark needs 6 spot colours plus white; for budget runs use the simplified mark (4 colours: ring, green, white, blue) |
| **Embroidery** | Caps, beanies, quarter-zips | Full icon has too much detail — embroider the **simplified mark only**, 55–65 mm, satin stitch ring, no gradient attempts |
| **DTG / DTF** | Small runs, photographic designs | Fine for everything; colour-match to a proof |

#### Stickers

The workhorse format. Spec for the standard die-cut:

- **Shape:** circle, following the mark's outer ring — the die line is a circle at the ring's outer edge plus 2 mm
- **Sizes:** 76 mm (3″) standard, 50 mm (2″) small; use the full icon on both, the simplified mark below 40 mm
- **Bleed:** extend the ring colour `#C8CDD0` 3 mm past the die line; 3 mm safe zone inside it
- **Material:** matte vinyl, laminated — these live on water bottles, tailgates, and ski bases; gloss looks cheap and glare kills the mark outdoors
- **Also make:** a stacked-lockup rectangle sticker, 90 × 70 mm with 4 mm corner radius, dark variant on Forest Deep — reads as a badge

#### Rack cards

4″ × 9″, 100 lb cover, matte. Front: photographic hero with the dark horizontal lockup over a dimmed image. Back: the four proof points, one QR code to the site, nothing else. The card's job is one scan, not a brochure's worth of copy.

### Search & discovery

- Alt text on every image, logo included.
- Compress everything; mobile page speed is the constraint that matters.
- Keep the logo in the same position across all platforms — recognition is built by repetition.
- The brand name appears in the wild as *bestseatosky*, *best seatosky*, and *Best SeaToSky*. The site already targets these variants. Write it as **Best Sea to Sky** in all copy, but don't correct people who type it closed up.

## Tools

**Design** — Adobe Illustrator for logo and vector work; Photoshop for photography and resizing. Figma is a workable free alternative for layout and mockups.

**Colour & type** — Adobe Color for testing palette extensions; Google Fonts for DM Serif Display and Source Sans 3; any contrast checker before you commit a text-on-colour combination.

**Storage** — Google Drive or Dropbox, one master brand folder, shared access.

**Social** — Blotato for scheduling across Facebook and Instagram. Keep this brand book's voice section handy when writing post copy.
