# 🌲 Milo Team Collaboration & Decision Protocol

> **The Operating Standard for Coordinated Milo Content Production.**  
> *Enforced by Best Sea to Sky (`bestseatosky.com`). Canon Gatekeeper: Rick Judson.*

---

## 🏛️ 1. Team Collaboration & Non-Negotiable Continuity Rules

All agents working on Milo must operate as one coordinated production team. Review the approved character specifications, script, voice reference, visual reference, factual-accuracy requirements, and prior production decisions before beginning work.

### Non-Negotiable Continuity Rules
Milo’s approved voice, appearance, personality, clothing, logo placement, speaking style, pacing, and factual-accuracy standards must remain consistent. Governed by [`MILO_VOICE_LOCK.md`](./MILO_VOICE_LOCK.md).

**Do not silently:**
* Split a scene or dialogue into multiple segments.
* Shorten or rewrite approved dialogue.
* Change voice models, presets, prompts, or settings (Fenrir is the sole authorized voice; Charon, Sven, ElevenLabs, Zephyr, Puck are strictly prohibited).
* Use the alternate `MILO_SLOW_GRAVELLY` mood without Rick's explicit approval.
* Apply pitch-shifting or artificial voice conversion.
* Substitute visual assets or generation models.
* Change Milo’s appearance, wardrobe, personality, accent, tone, or delivery.
* Make a technical compromise that could affect the finished result.

---

## 🤝 2. Team Consultation Before Asking Rick

Before asking Rick a question, the relevant agents must consult one another and determine:
1. What is uncertain or technically constrained?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What viable options are available?
4. What are the quality, continuity, cost, and production tradeoffs?
5. Which option does the team recommend?

Agents must share relevant information with one another. No agent may make an isolated decision that affects another production stage without informing the agents responsible for that stage (e.g. script, voice, visual-generation, lip-sync, editing, and quality-control agents must coordinate before production begins).

---

## 🛑 3. When to Ask Rick

Stop and ask Rick before proceeding whenever uncertainty or a proposed change could affect:
* Quality
* Cost or credit usage
* Milo’s voice or appearance
* Brand consistency
* Factual accuracy
* The approved script or production plan
* Scene length or number of segments
* Compatibility between production tools

Routine, reversible decisions may be handled independently. Bundle related uncertainties into one concise decision brief. Do not interrupt Rick with several fragmented questions.

---

## 📋 4. Required Decision Brief Protocol

When approval is required, present:

* **Issue:** What constraint or uncertainty was identified.
* **Option A:** Shorten or revise the script.
* **Option B:** Split the production while applying strict voice, visual, pacing, and transition controls.
* **Option C:** Use a different generation or lip-sync method that can preserve the approved scene.
* **Team Recommendation:** State the preferred option and explain why.
* **Impact:** Identify any effect on quality, continuity, cost, credits, timing, or factual accuracy.

*Wait for Rick’s decision before implementing any material change.*

---

## ⏱️ 5. Milo Scene-Length Rule

If a Milo scene exceeds a tool’s supported or reliable duration, do not automatically split it.

First, determine whether the best result would come from:
1. Tightening the script without weakening the message.
2. Producing multiple segments under locked continuity settings.
3. Using a different tool or workflow capable of producing the scene intact.

Present all viable options to Rick with one recommendation. Proceed only after receiving his choice.

---

## 🔍 6. Quality-Control Gate

Before delivering a Milo production, compare it against the approved master references and verify:
* Voice match (Canonical Fenrir profile & task benchmarks)
* Appearance and wardrobe match (Faded blue coveralls, MILO patch, Best Sea to Sky cap, signature wrench)
* Personality and delivery match (Deadpan, unhurried, grounded Squamish local)
* Script accuracy
* Lip-sync quality
* Segment continuity
* Logo and branding accuracy
* Factual accuracy (Zero hallucinations)
* Audio and visual quality

*If a material mismatch is found, do not call the production complete. Report the issue and recommend the corrective action.*
